The Case of the Missing Eight Hundred

Case briefing · Read first

A death at Willowmere Lodge

At 4:40 p.m., charity-auction chair Martin Vale was found dead in the lodge study, struck with a heavy brass bookend. Earlier that afternoon he had discovered that £800 of auction donations was unaccounted for.

Three people were near the study: Clara Finch, the treasurer; Felix Reed, the maintenance manager; and Nina Hart, the auction coordinator. Each insists they are innocent.

Investigate all three files. Each file reveals a different piece of the truth. Then submit an accusation with suspect, motive, and proof. Work together; nothing needs to be collected or reset.

Evidence A · Witness Statements

Words at the Door

Clara Finch: “I went to the study at 4:05 to leave the signed donation summary. Martin was alive and told me he wanted to review an expense. I left immediately.”

Nina Hart: “At about 4:20 I saw Felix walk toward the study carrying his tool bag. I thought Martin had called him about the loose cabinet hinge.”

Felix Reed: “I spent the entire time from four until half past four in the greenhouse. I never approached the study that afternoon.”

Compare what Nina says she saw at about 4:20 with Felix's account of where he was.

Case closed · The evidence speaks

An inconvenient repair

Felix Reed is the strongest suspect. The invented £800 repair expense gives him a plausible financial motive. Nina saw him heading toward the study around 4:20, and Felix's card opened its door at 4:19—despite his claim that he remained in the greenhouse and never went near it.

The case supports a reasoned accusation, not laboratory certainty: someone could have used his card, and the medical time window is approximate. A proper police inquiry would still need to corroborate the card's use and investigate physical evidence.
