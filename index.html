The Blue Vase

A death at Willowmere Lodge. Three competing stories. One detail that does not belong.

An original, spoiler-free tabletop mystery for a book club. Not an adaptation of the novel. Designed for 2–4 detectives per team, with paper or phone.

Read before opening evidence

The case

On the afternoon of the charity auction, Martin Vale discovered that a greenhouse-repair charge had grown from £80 to £800. He intended to question the people who handled the accounts.

At 4:36 p.m., auction coordinator Nina Hart entered Martin’s private study and found him fatally injured. A heavy brass bookend lay nearby; a blue-and-gold ceramic vase had shattered on the floor. Staff arrived at 4:40.

Your task: Decide who most likely killed Martin, identify a convincing false lead, and connect evidence from more than one file. No single file settles the case.

Clara FinchTreasurer. Responsible for the final auction accounts.
Felix ReedMaintenance manager. His initials appear beside the suspicious repair charge.
Nina HartAuction coordinator. Feared the fundraiser might collapse.
Teams may read Files A–C in any order. The site never grades answers or discloses the solution. Your host has the sealed resolution. Keep a rival explanation in mind.

Evidence file A · Interview notes

Four accounts

Statements gathered on the evening of the charity auction. Reported times are approximate unless corroborated by a record.

CLARA FINCH · TREASURER

“I took the donation summary to Martin at about 4:05. He was annoyed about a figure but perfectly well. I returned to reception. I stepped away once for supplies; I did not go back into the study.”

FELIX REED · MAINTENANCE

“I went into the study at 4:19 to repair a cupboard hinge. I photographed the finished job at 4:21. Martin was alive when I left, and he complained that someone had got the repairs account wrong. Then I went to meet a delivery at the greenhouse.”

NINA HART · AUCTION COORDINATOR

“I was leading the raffle in the hall from about 4:24 until 4:34. I went to ask Martin to announce the last prize at 4:36. He was on the floor, and the blue vase was broken. I called for help immediately.”

JUNE BELL · RECEPTION VOLUNTEER

“Clara left the desk around 4:22 and returned at 4:31. She emptied some scraps into my wastebasket. I noticed a blue pottery chip painted with a little gold leaf. It seemed too pretty to throw away, so I set it aside. I didn't see where she got it.”

Detective notes · A

Need a gentle investigative nudge?

Pay attention to objects people handled, not only to where they say they were.

ACCOUNTING PROCEDURE · AUCTION COMMITTEE

Department leads initial paper invoices. The treasurer prepares and signs the final accounts from those invoices. Other volunteers may handle envelopes and cash, but the committee does not yet know where the additional £720 went.

Martin’s handwritten margin note: “Check original before approving total. Must ask about this difference.”

Detective notes · B

Need a gentle investigative nudge?

Ask which document someone signed, and who was responsible for making the final version.

Evidence file C · Clock-checked records

The minutes that matter

These records use the same clock. They show access and activity, not necessarily who held a shared pass or what happened inside the study.

4:05Clara’s assigned card opens the study.

4:19Felix’s assigned card opens the study.

4:21Felix takes the repair photograph shown below; the blue-and-gold vase is intact.

4:22Martin leaves a recorded message for the auction secretary: “The repair figure doesn't match the invoice. I'll speak to the treasurer.” He is clearly alive.

4:26Shared reception pass R2 opens the study. The pass was left at reception for staff errands; there is no record of who held it.

4:28Felix signs for a greenhouse delivery; the delivery driver countersigns. The greenhouse is about six minutes’ walk from the study.

4:26–4:33Raffle recordings show Nina speaking in the main hall, including the 4:26 and 4:32 prize announcements.

4:36Nina’s assigned card opens the study. She alerts staff; the vase is shattered and a small gold-leaf-painted chip is missing.

4:40Staff arrive. Martin is dead. The shared pass R2 has been returned to its reception hook.

Detective notes · C

Need a gentle investigative nudge?

Lay the events in chronological order. Then revisit an object mentioned in a witness statement.

Case conclusion · Team verdict

Make the accusation

The best detective doesn't merely choose a name. Your team should explain which independent records fit together and address an apparently damning fact that points somewhere else.
